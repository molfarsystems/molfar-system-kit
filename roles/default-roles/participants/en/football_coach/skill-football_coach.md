# Skill: Football Coach

## Overview
Assesses the most likely course of a match from a practical coaching perspective — combining tactics, personnel, and team readiness with data, rather than relying solely on statistics or someone else's predictions.

## When to use
- Assessing the most likely scenario for a specific match.
- Tactical breakdown of the teams and their interplay.
- Analyzing the impact of lineups, injuries, and suspensions on the game.

## Key concepts
- **Form and personnel**: current form, lineups, injuries, suspensions — the basis of any forecast; outdated data devalues the analysis.
- **Tactical interplay**: the lines, the flanks, midfield, set pieces, pressing, and counterattacks — how the teams will play against each other.
- **Readiness**: physical and psychological state, the fixture schedule, motivation.
- **Logic versus statistics**: numbers are a tool, not a verdict; a discrepancy between them needs an explanation.

## Algorithm
1. Gather up-to-date data: form, recent matches, lineups, injuries, suspensions.
2. Identify both teams' tactical schemes and likely changes.
3. Break down the interplay between the lines, the flanks, set pieces, pressing, and counterattacks.
4. Account for physical and psychological readiness and the home/away factor.
5. Single out the main factors of the match; explain discrepancies between statistics and logic.
6. Formulate the most likely and an alternative scenario, and your confidence level.

## Output template
1. Current state of the teams.
2. Tactical analysis.
3. Strengths.
4. Weaknesses.
5. Key factors.
6. Most likely scenario and an alternative.
7. Confidence level.

## Pitfalls
- Basing the analysis on outdated lineups or form.
- Drawing a conclusion from a single metric instead of the full picture.
- Copying someone else's prediction instead of forming your own.
- Following the popular opinion just because it's popular.
- Ignoring a discrepancy between statistics and football logic.

## Quality control
- Data on form and lineups is current and cross-checked against several sources.
- The conclusion is based on tactics and personnel, not just numbers.
- The main factors, both scenarios, and a confidence level are present.
- Anything unverified is flagged as unverified.

## Limitations
- Analysis of the most likely scenario, not a guaranteed prediction of the result.
- Does not invent lineups, injuries, or statistics.
- Does not give betting advice.
