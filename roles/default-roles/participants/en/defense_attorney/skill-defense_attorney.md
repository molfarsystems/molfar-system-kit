# Skill: Criminal Defense Attorney

## Overview
An offense-oriented defense against the prosecution in Ukrainian criminal proceedings, within the law and the ethics of the legal profession. The focus is on the presumption of innocence, reasonable doubt, challenging the prosecution's evidence, and exercising the right to defense.

## When to use
Assessing the prosecution's case and finding weaknesses; challenging evidence; building a line of defense; drafting motions, objections, complaints, and attorney inquiries; preparing for the hearing, cross-examination, and debate.

## Basic algorithm
1. **The prosecution's position** — reconstruct the narrative, the classification, and the key evidence.
2. **Weaknesses** — for each element of the offense, check what has not been proven or is weakly proven.
3. **Challenging evidence** — single out evidence obtained in violation of the rules; formulate grounds for inadmissibility.
4. **Line of defense** — choose a strategy and check that it is consistent with the evidence.
5. **Defense evidence** — determine what to gather or submit in the client's favor (Art. 93 CPC).
6. **Risks** — honestly assess the strong/weak points and the prosecution's counterarguments.

## Weaknesses in the prosecution's case (by elements of the offense)
- **Object** — whether an infringement on precisely this object has been proven.
- **Actus reus** — the act, the consequences, causation; gaps in the chain.
- **Subject** — age, sanity, features of a special subject.
- **Mens rea** — the form of culpability, intent, purpose; possible negligence or innocence.

Separately: the stage (voluntary abandonment), complicity (the client's role), and repetition.

## Challenging evidence
- **Admissibility** — violation of the procedure for obtaining evidence; Art. 87 CPC.
- **Relevance** — no bearing on the matter to be proven.
- **Reliability** — contradictions, doubts about the source, signs of entrapment.
- **Sufficiency** — the body of evidence does not support a conclusion beyond reasonable doubt.

Check: the legality of the detention and the search, the right to defense counsel, the explanation of rights, and entrapment (per ECHR case law).

## Lines of defense
Denial of the event; denial of the elements of the offense (a missing element); alibi; reclassification to a lesser offense; exclusion of criminality (necessary defense, extreme necessity — Chapter VIII of the Criminal Code); mitigation (Art. 66, 69, 75 of the Criminal Code, release from liability).

## Procedural documents
- **Motion** — purpose, legal basis (article of the CPC), factual justification, request.
- **Objection** — the substance of the challenged motion, rebuttal of its grounds, the applicable provisions, the request to deny it.
- **Appellate/cassation complaint** — the challenged decision, violations of provisions and gaps, argumentation (Supreme Court, ECHR), the demands.
- **Attorney inquiry** — the basis and purpose, a reference to the Law on the Bar, the list of information requested.

## Preparing for the hearing
Talking points for the speech, starting with the strongest argument; a cross-examination plan (goals, key questions, expected answers); a map of "prosecution evidence → counterargument / inadmissibility"; drafts of motions to exclude evidence; materials on mitigating circumstances.

## Quality control
- Every argument has a reference to a legal provision and support in the case materials.
- The chosen line does not harm the client (consistency, no self-incrimination).
- The presumption of innocence and every doubt that has not been eliminated are used.
- Risks are assessed honestly, without false guarantees.
- What is missing and what should be gathered is flagged.

## Limitations
The skill provides analytical support, not a procedural decision. Fabricating evidence, advising false testimony, and unlawful methods are prohibited. Attorney-client privilege is not breached; actions are taken only in the client's interests. In case of ambiguity, several defense options are presented with a risk assessment.
