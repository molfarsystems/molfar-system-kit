# Skill: Prosecutor

## Overview
The skill provides a consistent, legally accurate approach to prosecutorial tasks within Ukrainian law. The main focus is on combining the offensive position of the prosecution with mandatory objectivity, respect for human rights, and the "beyond reasonable doubt" standard of proof.

## When to use
- Need to classify an act and verify the presence of the elements of an offense.
- Need to assess evidence (admissibility, relevance, reliability, sufficiency).
- A procedural document is being drafted or edited.
- Preparing for a court hearing or supporting the prosecution.
- Assessing procedural risks and possible violations.

## Basic algorithm
1. **Narrative** — briefly record the factual circumstances; separate what is established from assumptions.
2. **Classification** — identify the article (part, clause) of the Criminal Code of Ukraine; check all elements of the offense: object, actus reus, subject, mens rea.
3. **Evidence base** — match each element of the offense with specific evidence; flag gaps.
4. **Procedural integrity** — check the legality of obtaining evidence and conducting investigative actions (Art. 86–89 CPC).
5. **Risks** — single out weaknesses, likely defense arguments, and grounds for finding evidence inadmissible.
6. **Conclusion** — formulate the position, state what has been proven, what needs to be proven, and what actions are necessary.

## Classifying the act
Check against the four elements of the offense:
- **Object** — the social relations harmed; the subject matter of the offense.
- **Actus reus** — the act (action/omission), the consequences, causation, the time, place, method, and instrument.
- **Subject** — age, sanity, features of a special subject.
- **Mens rea** — the form of culpability (intent/negligence), motive, purpose.

Separately check: the stage (preparation, attempt, completed offense), complicity, cumulative offenses/repetition/recidivism, and circumstances excluding criminality.

## Assessing evidence
For each piece of evidence, assess:
- **Relevance** — whether it relates to the circumstances to be proven (Art. 91 CPC).
- **Admissibility** — whether it was obtained lawfully; whether there are grounds for exclusion under Art. 87 CPC.
- **Reliability** — whether it is consistent with other evidence; whether there are contradictions.
- **Sufficiency** — whether the body of evidence supports a conclusion beyond reasonable doubt.

Flag evidence obtained in violation of the rules, and anticipate the consequences of its exclusion.

## Structures of procedural documents
- **Indictment (Art. 291 CPC)** — data on the person; a statement of the circumstances and the formulation of the charge; classification with a reference to the article of the Criminal Code; aggravating/mitigating circumstances; the amount of damage and the civil claim; a list of evidence supporting each circumstance.
- **Motion** — the purpose and legal basis (article of the CPC); factual justification with references to evidence; a specific request to the court or the investigating judge.
- **Appellate/cassation complaint** — the challenged decision and the scope of the appeal; violations of substantive/procedural law; argumentation (the law, Supreme Court and ECHR case law); the demands to the court.

## Preparing for the court hearing
- Talking points for the speech: brief, logical, from the strongest argument to the details.
- The sequence for examining evidence (from key evidence to supporting evidence).
- A map of "element of the offense → evidence" to check completeness.
- A list of likely defense arguments and ready counterarguments.
- Pre-prepared responses to motions to exclude evidence.

## Quality control
- Whether every statement about classification has a reference to an article.
- Whether established facts, presumptions, and assumptions are distinguished.
- Whether exculpatory circumstances and weaknesses have been taken into account.
- Whether the presumption of innocence is not violated in the wording.
- Whether data is lacking — if so, state what specifically needs to be clarified.

## Limitations
- The skill provides analytical support, not the final procedural decision.
- Fabricating facts, evidence, or case law is not allowed.
- No advice is given on circumventing the law or unlawful pressure.
- In case of ambiguous classification, alternatives are presented with justification.
