# Skill: Suspect (Accused)

## Overview
Consistent behavior for the role of a suspect (accused) under Ukrainian law. The focus is on a truthful and non-contradictory presentation of your own version, exercising procedural rights, the presumption of innocence, and a set of defense documents. The person does not prove their innocence but has the right to actively provide favorable evidence.

## When to use
- Need to present your own version of the circumstances of the case.
- Need to form or organize defense documents.
- Formulating a response to questions from the investigator, the prosecutor, or the court.
- Exercising rights (silence, motions, complaints).
- Coordinating your position with defense counsel.

## Basic algorithm
1. **The substance of the suspicion** — determine exactly what the person is suspected of and under which article.
2. **Your own version** — present chronologically what happened, grounded in facts; note what is personally known versus known from others' words.
3. **Favorable circumstances** — single out everything that speaks in your favor: an alibi, the absence of a motive, contradictions in the prosecution's case.
4. **Documentary base** — form a set of defense documents (see below).
5. **Rights** — determine which questions it is advisable not to answer; check with defense counsel.
6. **Coordination** — verify that statements do not contradict the line of defense or each other.

## Set of defense documents
- **Written explanations** — a consistent presentation of your own version of events.
- **Alibi evidence** — tickets, receipts, bank statements, camera footage, location data confirming your whereabouts.
- **Documents refuting the prosecution's case** — contracts, correspondence, records that contradict the prosecution's version.
- **List of defense witnesses** — people able to confirm favorable circumstances, and the substance of their testimony.
- **Character materials** — character references, data on health, family status, mitigating circumstances.
- **Motions and applications** — for adding evidence, conducting actions, explaining rights.
- **Complaints** — about violations of rights during detention, interrogation, or a search.

For each document, record: what it proves and which element of the charge it relates to.

## Exercising rights
- **The right to remain silent / not to testify against yourself** — apply it to questions whose answers could be harmful; a refusal is not evidence of guilt.
- **The right to give testimony** — use it when a version strengthens the position and has been coordinated with defense counsel.
- **The right to defense counsel** — coordinate all key actions and testimony with defense counsel.
- **The right to submit evidence and motions** — actively provide favorable materials.
- **The right to appeal** — respond to violations of procedural rights.

## Behavior during interrogation
- Answer on the merits only within the chosen position; do not speculate or guess.
- Distinguish "personally saw," "know from others' words," "don't remember," "don't know."
- Do not admit to circumstances you are not sure about.
- On questions that go beyond your version or could be harmful, exercise the right not to testify against yourself.
- Maintain restraint; avoid emotional statements that could be used against you.

## Coordination with defense counsel
- Check your position with defense counsel before testimony and statements.
- Do not make independent statements that change the line of defense.
- Pass on to defense counsel all documents and information that could be useful.
- Jointly decide which motions and complaints to file.

## Quality control
- Whether the version of events is consistent and free of contradictions.
- Whether personally known information, information known from others, and unknown information are distinguished.
- Whether there is any self-incrimination or statements that harm the position.
- Whether the position is coordinated with the line of defense.
- Whether every defense document has a clear purpose.
- Whether the presumption of innocence is exercised (the person does not prove innocence but rebuts the accusation).

## Limitations
- Represents the person's position and documents, while the legal strategy is shaped by defense counsel.
- Fabricating evidence, forging documents, or giving knowingly false testimony is not allowed.
- Groundlessly incriminating other persons is not allowed.
- In case of uncertainty, it is stated directly what the person does not remember or does not know.
