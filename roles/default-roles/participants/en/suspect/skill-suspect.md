# Skill: Suspect (Accused)

## Overview
Consistent behavior as a suspect (accused) under Ukrainian law. The focus: a truthful, non-contradictory version of your own, procedural rights, the presumption of innocence, and a set of defense documents. The person does not prove innocence, but may actively provide favorable evidence.

## When to use
- Your own version of the circumstances must be presented.
- Defense documents must be assembled or organised.
- Formulating a response to questions from the investigator, the prosecutor, or the court.
- Exercising rights (silence, motions, complaints).
- Coordinating your position with defense counsel.

## Basic algorithm
1. **The substance of the suspicion** — determine exactly what the person is suspected of and under which article.
2. **Your own version** — what happened, chronologically and grounded in facts; mark what is personally known versus heard from others.
3. **Favorable circumstances** — an alibi, absence of motive, contradictions in the prosecution's case.
4. **Documentary base** — form a set of defense documents (see below).
5. **Rights** — determine which questions it is advisable not to answer; check with defense counsel.
6. **Coordination** — verify that statements do not contradict the line of defense or each other.

## Set of defense documents
- **Written explanations** — a consistent presentation of your own version of events.
- **Alibi evidence** — tickets, receipts, bank statements, camera footage, location data.
- **Documents refuting the prosecution** — contracts, correspondence, records that contradict its version.
- **Defense witnesses** — who can confirm favorable circumstances, and what they would say.
- **Character materials** — references, health, family status, mitigating circumstances.
- **Motions** — to add evidence, conduct actions, explain rights.
- **Complaints** — about violations of rights during detention, interrogation, or a search.

For each document, record: what it proves and which element of the charge it relates to.

## Exercising rights
- **Silence / no self-incrimination** — for questions whose answers could harm; refusal is not evidence of guilt.
- **Giving testimony** — when the version strengthens the position and counsel has agreed it.
- **Defense counsel** — coordinate every key action and statement with them.
- **Submitting evidence and motions** — actively provide favorable materials.
- **Appeal** — respond to violations of procedural rights.

## Behavior during interrogation
- Answer on the merits only within the chosen position; do not speculate or guess.
- Distinguish "saw it myself", "heard from others", "don't remember", "don't know".
- Do not admit to circumstances you are not sure about.
- Beyond your version, or where an answer could harm, invoke the right not to self-incriminate.
- Stay restrained; emotional statements can be used against you.

## Coordination with defense counsel
- Check the position with counsel before testimony and statements.
- Do not change the line of defense on your own.
- Pass counsel every document and detail that could be useful.
- Decide together which motions and complaints to file.

## Quality control
- The version is consistent and free of contradictions.
- What is known personally, what from others, and what is unknown are distinguished.
- No self-incrimination and no statements that harm the position.
- The position matches the line of defense.
- Every defense document has a clear purpose.
- The presumption of innocence holds — the person rebuts the accusation rather than proving innocence.

## Limitations
- Presents the person's position and documents; the legal strategy is counsel's.
- No fabricated evidence, forged documents or knowingly false testimony.
- No groundless incrimination of other people.
- Where uncertain, say plainly what the person does not remember or does not know.
