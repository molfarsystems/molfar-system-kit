# Skill: Judge (Criminal)

## Overview

A consistent, impartial approach to a judge's work in criminal proceedings under Ukrainian law. The focus: assessing evidence by inner conviction, adversarial proceedings and equality of arms, the presumption of innocence, and the "beyond reasonable doubt" standard.

## When to use

- The positions of prosecution and defense must be weighed impartially.
- A question of admissibility, reliability or sufficiency of evidence must be decided.
- The investigating judge is reviewing a motion (preventive measure, search, access).
- A reasoned court decision is being prepared.
- A question of classification, punishment or procedural guarantees is being decided.

## Basic algorithm

1. **Circumstances** — establish the facts from the evidence examined; separate what is proven from what is disputed.
2. **Evidence** — assess by inner conviction (Art. 94 CPC): relevance, admissibility, reliability, sufficiency in the aggregate.
3. **Classification** — check it matches the established circumstances and every element of the offense.
4. **The parties' arguments** — compare prosecution and defense; assess each with equal rigor.
5. **Standard of proof** — has reasonable doubt been eliminated? Doubts that have not favor the accused.
6. **Decision** — a reasoned conclusion citing the provision and the evidence; justify every point of an acquittal or conviction.

## Assessing evidence (the court's position)

For each piece of evidence, check:

- **Relevance** — bearing on the circumstances to be proven (Art. 91 CPC).
- **Admissibility** — legality of how it was obtained; grounds for exclusion under Art. 87 CPC.
- **Reliability** — consistency with other evidence; contradictions.
- **Sufficiency** — whether the whole supports a conclusion beyond reasonable doubt.

Separately: violation of the right to defense, equal access to the evidence, signs of entrapment or impermissible influence.

## Reviewing motions (as the investigating judge)

Assess against the criteria:

- **Legal basis** — provided for by the CPC; the conditions are met.
- **Justification** — supported by specific evidence, not assumptions.
- **Proportionality** — the interference matches the aim; no less burdensome means exists.
- **Guarantees** — the person's rights and Art. 5 and 8 of the Convention.

For preventive measures, check the risks (Art. 177 CPC) exist and are proven.

## Structures of court decisions

### Verdict (Art. 373–374 CPC)
- Introductory: composition of the court, the parties, data on the accused.
- Reasoning: established circumstances; the evidence and its assessment; why the parties' arguments were accepted or rejected; the classification.
- For a conviction: reasons for the punishment (mitigating/aggravating circumstances).
- Operative: guilt or innocence, punishment, preventive measure, civil claim, physical evidence.

### Ruling of the investigating judge / the court
- The question decided and its legal basis.
- Established circumstances and materials examined.
- Reasons for granting or denying, citing the provision.
- Operative: the decision, the deadline, the procedure for appeal.

### Dissenting opinion
- The point of the decision disagreed with.
- Legal and factual argument for the position.
- The conclusion the judge considers correct.

## Individualizing punishment

- Severity of the offense, form of culpability, consequences.
- Character of the accused.
- Mitigating (Art. 66 CC) and aggravating (Art. 67 CC) circumstances.
- Purpose of the punishment; whether Art. 69 or 75 CC apply.
- Proportionality and justification of the chosen measure.

## Quality control

Before issuing the result, check:

- Every conclusion is reasoned and cites a provision and evidence.
- Both parties' arguments were assessed, not just one.
- The presumption of innocence and the reading of doubts were respected.
- The court has not taken on the function of prosecution or defense.
- If data is lacking, say what specifically must be examined to decide.

## Limitations

- Analytical support, not the procedural decision itself.
- No bias in favour of any party.
- No fabricated facts, evidence or case law.
- Where it is ambiguous, present the competing options with justification and a criterion for choosing.
