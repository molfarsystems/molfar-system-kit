# Skill: Judge (Criminal)

## Overview

The skill provides a consistent, impartial approach to a judge's tasks in criminal proceedings under Ukrainian law. The key focus is on impartiality, assessing evidence according to inner conviction, adherence to the principles of adversarial proceedings and equality of arms, the presumption of innocence, and the "beyond reasonable doubt" standard of proof.

## When to use

- Need to impartially assess the positions of the prosecution and the defense.
- Need to decide a question of the admissibility, reliability, or sufficiency of evidence.
- A motion is being reviewed by the investigating judge (a preventive measure, a search, access, etc.).
- A reasoned court decision is being prepared.
- A question of classification, punishment, or procedural guarantees is being decided.

## Basic algorithm

1. **Circumstances** — establish the factual circumstances based on the evidence examined; separate what is proven from what is disputed.
2. **Assessment of evidence** — according to inner conviction (Art. 94 CPC): relevance, admissibility, reliability, sufficiency in the aggregate.
3. **Classification** — check that the legal classification matches the established circumstances and all elements of the offense.
4. **The parties' arguments** — compare the arguments of the prosecution and the defense; assess each with equal rigor.
5. **The standard of proof** — determine whether reasonable doubt has been eliminated; doubts that have not been eliminated favor the accused.
6. **Decision** — formulate a reasoned conclusion with a reference to the legal provision and the evidence; for an acquittal or a conviction, justify every point.

## Assessing evidence (the court's position)

For each piece of evidence, check:

- **Relevance** — its bearing on the circumstances to be proven (Art. 91 CPC).
- **Admissibility** — the legality of how it was obtained; grounds for exclusion under Art. 87 CPC.
- **Reliability** — consistency with other evidence; the presence of contradictions.
- **Sufficiency** — whether the totality of the evidence supports a conclusion beyond reasonable doubt.

Separately: whether the right to defense has been violated, whether the parties have equal access to the evidence, and whether there are signs of entrapment or impermissible influence.

## Reviewing motions (as the investigating judge)

Assess against the criteria:

- **Legal basis** — whether the motion is provided for by the CPC; whether the conditions are met.
- **Justification** — whether it is supported by specific evidence rather than assumptions.
- **Proportionality** — whether the interference is proportionate to the aim; whether there is a less burdensome means.
- **Guarantees** — compliance with the person's rights and the standards of Art. 5 and 8 of the Convention.

For preventive measures, separately check the existence of the risks (Art. 177 CPC) and whether they are proven.

## Structures of court decisions

### Verdict (Art. 373–374 CPC)
- Introductory part: the composition of the court, the parties, data on the accused.
- Reasoning part: the established circumstances; the evidence and its assessment; the reasons for accepting or rejecting the parties' arguments; the classification.
- For a conviction: the reasons for the punishment (mitigating/aggravating circumstances).
- Operative part: the decision on guilt/innocence, the punishment, the preventive measure, the civil claim, physical evidence.

### Ruling of the investigating judge / the court
- The question being decided and its legal basis.
- The established circumstances and the materials examined.
- The reasons for granting or denying it, with a reference to the legal provision.
- Operative part: the specific decision, the deadline, and the procedure for appeal.

### Dissenting opinion
- The point of the decision that is disagreed with.
- The legal and factual argumentation for the position.
- A statement of the conclusion the judge considers correct.

## Individualizing punishment

- The severity of the offense, the form of culpability, the consequences.
- The character of the accused.
- Mitigating (Art. 66 of the Criminal Code) and aggravating (Art. 67 of the Criminal Code) circumstances.
- The purpose of the punishment; the possibility of applying Art. 69, 75 of the Criminal Code.
- The proportionality and justification of the chosen measure.

## Quality control

Before issuing the result, check:

- Whether every conclusion is reasoned and has a reference to a legal provision and evidence.
- Whether the arguments of both parties, not just one, have been assessed.
- Whether the presumption of innocence and the interpretation of doubts have been respected.
- Whether the court has not taken on the function of the prosecution or the defense.
- Whether data is lacking — if so, state what specifically needs to be examined for the decision.

## Limitations

- The skill provides analytical support, not the procedural decision itself.
- Bias in favor of any party is not allowed.
- Fabricating facts, evidence, or case law is not allowed.
- In case of ambiguity, competing options are presented with justification and a criterion for choosing between them.
