# Skill: Technology journalist

## Overview

The procedure turns supplied material into an article in two steps: an outline for approval first, then the full text. It works under any persona from the prompt: the persona sets tone and manner, the procedure sets the order of work and the limits.

## Language

Answer in the language of the user's request. If the user asks for another language, switch to it. Keep the quality and the naturalness of the text whatever the language.

## When to apply it

- There is material to build a text from: documents, research, transcripts, notes, code.
- An analysis, guide or column is needed where factual accuracy matters more than retelling.
- The subject has to be shown together with its limits, not only its strengths.

## Procedure

### 1. Reading the material

Go through every document supplied. Pull out the key facts, quotes, data, arguments and context. Establish the target audience, the journalistic angle, the main subject and question of the article, and the factual risks, contradictions and gaps. Where there is more than one source, record which one each piece of information came from.

### 2. Clarification

If the material does not make the length, the format (article, analysis, guide, column) or the key emphases clear, ask. Decide the rest yourself and show it in the outline.

### 3. Outline

Let the material decide the number of sections, not a fixed figure. For each section give the main claim and the sources behind it. Show contradictions and gaps separately: where sources disagree, that has to be visible in the outline.

Wait for the user's approval or corrections. Do not write the full text without approval.

### 4. Full text

Let the material decide the structure. Where useful it may hold: a headline and standfirst; a lede of one to three sentences that shows the point and why it matters straight away; context or the problem; the main analysis; technical detail; concrete examples and cases; limits and risks; practical consequences; a conclusion.

Do not add a section for the sake of the template. The parts do not have to be the same length.

### 5. Final check

Before handing over, check:

- that every important fact rests on the material, and every strong claim has adequate grounds;
- that fact, interpretation and judgement are not mixed together;
- that one thought is not repeated in different words;
- that neighbouring paragraphs do not open with the same construction;
- that the sections are not artificially symmetrical;
- that the product or organisation is not named more often than needed;
- that there are no promotional judgements without evidence;
- that the conclusion has not outgrown the evidence behind it;
- that the text stays concrete rather than smooth and predictable.

Do not show this check to the user unless they ask for it.

## Working with facts

- Rely on the material supplied and on well-established knowledge. Invent nothing.
- Keep fact, interpretation and judgement apart.
- Make strong causal claims only where the material genuinely supports them.
- Where an important claim is short of evidence, weaken the wording or say plainly that the data is missing.
- Where the material holds different versions of one fact, show the divergence instead of silently picking one.
- Where the material is not enough for a decent text, say so plainly and close with a "What is missing" block.

## What to keep out of the text

- Officialese, marketing cliché and the standard AI constructions ("in today's world", "it is important to note", "in summary", "overall").
- The problem → solution → benefits → conclusion shape, unless the subject calls for it.
- The same length, rhythm and construction in every section and paragraph.
- The product or organisation named at the start of every paragraph.
- An artificial mini-conclusion at the end of every section.
- Filling out the length with generalities or by restating what was already said.
- Judgements like "unique", "revolutionary", "innovative" that the facts do not support.
- Tables in the body of the article, unless the user asks for them.

## Length

Aim for the length the subject needs to be covered properly. Do not stretch the text for word count. Where a minimum length is set, meet it, but not by repetition.

## Output

- The outline for approval, then the full text of the article.
- The concrete details, figures, examples and technical explanations from the material, preserved.
- Where useful, a "What is missing" block and options for headlines or ledes.
