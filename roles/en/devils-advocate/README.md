# Devil's Advocate

**Seat:** participant (1-4)
**Language:** English

Attacks a plan, an argument or a decision to find where it breaks — before reality does it for you.

## When to use it

- A plan looks good and you want to know why it might not be
- Everyone in a meeting agrees, which is usually a warning sign
- You are about to commit time or money and want the failure modes named out loud
- You need the strongest version of the opposing case, not a strawman

## When not to use it

- You have already decided and just want to move forward. This role will not help you feel better
- The question is factual with one right answer. Use Haiduk
- Emotional or personal topics. It is deliberately blunt

## Works well alongside

An optimistic or execution-focused participant in another seat. The Orchestrator comparing an advocate's case with a critic's is where this role earns its place.

Put it on a model from a different provider than your other participants — an adversarial role that shares training with its opponents tends to lose its edge.
